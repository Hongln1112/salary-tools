How minimum wage works in the US

The federal minimum wage has been $7.25/hour since 2009. Many states set a higher minimum wage — when a state rate is higher than the federal rate, employers must pay the higher one. This is called the "higher of" rule.

  • Federal floor: $7.25/hour (2026, unchanged since 2009)
  • State minimums: range from $7.25 to over $17/hour, and many adjust yearly for inflation
  • Cities: Seattle ($20.76), New York City ($16.00+), and other major cities set even higher local minimums

What if my pay is below the minimum?

If your calculated hourly rate falls below the minimum for your state, that may mean your pay is not compliant. You should speak with your employer or your state's labor department. Note that exceptions apply to certain job categories — tipped workers, students, and some small businesses have different rules. Most non-exempt hourly workers are covered.

Is minimum wage per month or per year?

Minimum wage is stated per hour. To work out the annual minimum, multiply the hourly rate by 2,080 hours (40 hours/week × 52 weeks). For example, $15/hour × 2,080 = $31,200/year before taxes. After taxes and FICA, that's roughly $25,000–$27,000 in take-home pay depending on your state.
