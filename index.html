Common questions

At a standard 40-hour, 52-week schedule, $50,000 a year breaks down to roughly $24.04 per hour before taxes. That figure assumes you work 2,080 hours annually with no unpaid time off. If you work fewer weeks (say, 50) or fewer hours per week, your effective hourly rate goes up. After federal income tax, FICA, and a typical state tax, your actual take-home hourly rate is closer to $18–20 depending on which state you live in. Use the Take-Home Pay Calculator for an exact after-tax number.

Gross pay is the full amount your employer agrees to pay you — the number you see on your job offer letter. Net pay, also called take-home pay, is what actually lands in your bank account after deductions. Those deductions typically include federal income tax, state income tax (in most states), FICA (Social Security at 6.2% and Medicare at 1.45%), and any pre-tax benefits like health insurance or 401(k) contributions. The gap between gross and net can easily be 20–35% depending on your income level and state. Use the Take-Home Pay Calculator to see your exact breakdown.

The formula is simple: divide your annual salary by your total working hours in a year. For a standard full-time schedule, that's hours per week × 52 weeks. So 40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 working hours per year. If you earn $75,000 annually, your hourly rate is $75,000 ÷ 2,080 = $36.06. Keep in mind this is your gross hourly rate — before taxes, benefits, and any unpaid time off are factored in. The quick converter above handles this math instantly.

Under the US Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), non-exempt employees must receive at least 1.5× their regular hourly rate for any hours worked beyond 40 in a single workweek. This is called time-and-a-half. Some states go further: California, for example, requires double-time (2×) for hours beyond 12 in a workday or beyond 8 on the seventh consecutive day of work. Salaried employees may still be entitled to overtime depending on their duties and salary level — it's not automatically waived just because you're paid a salary. The Overtime Calculator handles both time-and-a-half and double-time scenarios.

No — freelancers and independent contractors pay significantly more in self-employment tax. As a W-2 employee, you pay 7.65% in FICA and your employer matches another 7.65%. As a freelancer, you pay both sides yourself: 15.3% of your net earnings on top of regular income tax. You're also responsible for making estimated quarterly tax payments (April, June, September, January) rather than having taxes withheld from a paycheck. When setting your freelance rates, the Freelance Rate Calculator factors in these extra self-employment costs so you don't underprice yourself.
