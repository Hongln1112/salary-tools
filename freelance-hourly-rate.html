Why freelancers need a different rate formula

As a freelancer or contractor, your hourly rate needs to cover far more than an employee's salary. You must factor in:

  • Self-employment tax — you pay both the employee and employer side of FICA (15.3% total).
  • Business expenses — software, tools, home office, health insurance, accounting software.
  • Vacation & sick leave — you don't get paid time off, so you must bill for it in your rate.
  • Non-billable hours — emails, marketing, invoicing, and admin don't directly earn money.

Example

If you want $60,000 take-home, pay 25% in combined tax, spend $5,000 on business expenses, bill 25 hours/week, and take 4 weeks vacation, your required rate works out to roughly $90–$100/hour. Employees in comparable roles often earn the equivalent of $40–$50/hour — freelancers charge more because they bear all these costs themselves.
