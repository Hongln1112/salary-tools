How overtime pay works

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), most hourly employees earn 1.5 times their regular rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek — this is called time-and-a-half. Some jobs, holidays or agreements use double time (2×) instead.

Weekly pay = (Regular hours × Rate) + (Overtime hours × Rate × Multiplier)

Example: a $20/hour rate, 40 regular hours and 5 overtime hours at 1.5× gives $950 that week ($800 regular + $150 overtime). Over a year, that's roughly $7,800 in extra overtime pay.

Overtime FAQs

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, overtime is 1.5× your regular hourly rate for hours worked beyond 40 in a single workweek. Some states — like California — require double-time (2×) for hours beyond 12 in a workday or beyond 8 on the seventh consecutive workday. It's calculated weekly, not daily, under federal rules.
