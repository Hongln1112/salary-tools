How to calculate your hourly rate

Hourly rate = Annual salary ÷ (Hours per week × Weeks per year)

A salary of $52,000 at 40 hours per week and 52 weeks per year equals about $25.00 per hour. Use the calculator above with your own numbers.

The federal minimum wage is $7.25 per hour, but many states set a higher minimum — for example, Washington State's 2026 minimum wage is $17.13/hour. Check where you work.

Hourly wage FAQs

Divide your annual salary by the total hours you work in a year. For a standard 40-hour week over 52 weeks, that's 2,080 hours. So $52,000 ÷ 2,080 = $25/hour. Adjust the hours per week if you work fewer hours. After federal, state, and FICA taxes, your take-home hourly rate will be roughly 20–35% lower.

The federal minimum wage is $7.25/hour, but 30+ states have set higher minimums. Washington State's is $17.13/hour in 2026. Your state's rate applies where you work, not necessarily where you live. Some cities also set their own higher local minimum wage.

This calculator shows a before-tax rate. To see what you actually take home after federal income tax, state income tax, and FICA (Social Security + Medicare), use the Take-Home Pay Calculator. The gap between gross and net hourly rate can easily be $5–10/hour depending on your income level and state.

Overtime is typically paid at 1.5× (time-and-a-half) your regular rate after 40 hours in a workweek. Some states require double-time for hours beyond 12 in a day. The Overtime Calculator adds that in automatically for your weekly and annual pay.

This tool estimates hourly pay on a simple before-tax basis and does not account for taxes, overtime, deductions or benefits. Minimum wage figures may change; verify with your state labor department.
