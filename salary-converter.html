How the conversion works

Annual = Hourly × Hours/week × Weeks/year

From that single relationship, every other figure follows: Weekly = Annual ÷ 52, and Monthly = Annual ÷ 12. Pick whichever number you already know, and the converter fills in the rest.

Example: $25/hour, 40 hours a week and 52 weeks a year equals about $52,000 a year, $1,000 a week, and $4,333 a month. After taxes, your actual take-home monthly pay will be roughly 20–35% lower.

Salary conversion FAQs

Multiply your hourly rate by hours per week, then by weeks per year. $25/hour × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $52,000/year. Use a full-time 40-hour, 52-week setup for the standard figure. If you work part-time or take unpaid vacation, adjust the hours and weeks accordingly.

52 weeks is standard for full-time salaried work. If you get 2 weeks of unpaid time off, some people use 50 weeks. If you get paid vacation, you still use 52 because those weeks are paid. The key is to use the number of weeks you actually receive pay for.

This converter shows gross pay (before taxes) and uses annual ÷ 12 for simplicity. For the amount you actually keep after federal income tax, state income tax, and FICA, use the Take-Home Pay Calculator. The gap between gross monthly pay and net monthly pay can easily be $800–$1,500 depending on your income level.

Biweekly pay means 26 paychecks a year (27 in leap years with 53 Fridays), while semi-monthly is 24 paychecks. A biweekly check is roughly annual ÷ 26, so each check is smaller than a semi-monthly check of annual ÷ 24 — even though the total yearly pay is identical. Budgeting by monthly income, not per-check income, avoids confusion.

This tool converts gross pay between time periods on a simple basis and does not account for taxes, deductions or benefits. Monthly figures are annual ÷ 12 for simplicity.
