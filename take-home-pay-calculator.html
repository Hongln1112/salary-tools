What is take-home pay?

Take-home pay (or net pay) is the money left after federal income tax, FICA (Social Security + Medicare), and state income tax are withheld from your gross salary. It is what actually lands in your bank account.

Take-home = Gross − Federal − FICA − State

In 2026, FICA is 7.65% of wages (6.2% Social Security up to $184,500, plus 1.45% Medicare). The standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for head of household.

This calculator applies the standard deduction and 2026 federal brackets, then subtracts FICA and an estimated state tax. Where a state uses a single flat rate, that rate is used; where a state has progressive brackets, the tool uses a typical effective rate for a middle income earner. It is a solid estimate — not a substitute for a tax professional.

Take-home pay FAQs

Gross pay is your salary before any deductions. Take-home (net) pay is what's left after federal income tax, FICA (Social Security + Medicare) and state income tax are withheld. It's the money that actually lands in your bank account. The gap between gross and net can easily be 20–35% depending on your income level and which state you live in.

FICA is 7.65% of your wages in 2026 — 6.2% for Social Security (up to a wage base of $184,500) plus 1.45% for Medicare. Your employer matches this amount, so the total cost of your labor to your employer is roughly 7.65% above your stated salary. Self-employed freelancers pay both sides themselves — 15.3% total.

Nine states don't tax earned income: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington and Wyoming. You still pay federal income tax and FICA everywhere. Some of these states offset the lack of income tax with higher sales tax or property tax, so the overall cost of living varies.

Real withholding depends on your W-4 allowances, employer-sponsored health insurance, 401(k) contributions, HSA/FSA contributions, pre-tax commuter benefits, city or local income taxes, and tax credits like the Earned Income Tax Credit. This calculator uses standard federal brackets and approximate state rates for a solid ballpark — not a substitute for a tax professional.
