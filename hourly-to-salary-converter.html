Hourly to salary FAQs

Multiply your hourly rate by 40 (hours per week), then by 52 (weeks per year). Example: $20/hr × 40 hours × 52 weeks = $41,600/year before taxes. This assumes you work full-time and get paid vacation. After taxes and FICA, expect about 25–30% less in take-home pay.

52 is standard for salaried and hourly workers with paid time off. If you get 2 weeks of unpaid time off, use 50. If you get paid vacation, keep 52 — you're still earning during those weeks. The key is to count weeks where you actually receive a paycheck.

$20/hr = $41,600/year before taxes. That's above the US federal poverty line for a household of two, but tight in expensive cities like San Francisco or New York City where rent alone can take 40–50% of income. In most of the country, it's a solid entry-level or skilled-trade wage.

This calculator provides estimates for general guidance only. Actual take-home pay depends on taxes, deductions, overtime, and benefits. Always confirm your pay details with your employer.
